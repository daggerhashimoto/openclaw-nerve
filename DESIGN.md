# Design System: Nerve

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Night Light"**

An operator's console is a building seen at night. You do not read the facade by its architecture; you read it by which rooms are lit and how warm the light is. Nerve borrows that reading. Cold blue is an agent at work. Warm cantaloupe is the operator, and anything that has finished and now waits on the operator. When work is done the window goes back to its family colour: every room keeps its own light on at idle, so the building reads as inhabited, and only a lit pane changes, which is how work shows. Colour never decorates, it reports, and because every lit surface means something, the shell can hold a whole fleet in a glance without asking for attention.

Nerve ships that meaning in two worlds, and the meaning is written once in this file because both worlds share it. **Workbench** ("The Working Drawing") is the default for every install, in Workbench Night (graphite) and Workbench Paper. It is the console printed as a working drawing, adapted from the Ruled Workbench design system the user made for the Nerve landing page: rules are drawn as structure, floating surfaces print a faint hard offset, panes switch flat without bloom, a cool wash falls from the top of the room, paper grain lies over the shell, and the room is pinned to the frame by four diamond corner nodes. **Night light** is the console as a building lit at night, in Night and Daylight: plates read by value and a 1px edge light, a lit pane throws a short bloom, and the room carries an ambient light from above. Daylight is that building by day: cool paper, printed hairlines, panes printed as ink swatches. A browser that stored Night under the old default moves to Workbench Night once; picking Night again sticks.

Workbench lends type, palette, density and one signature move; layout, navigation, controls, the row unit and every component are the same in both worlds. The mood is exact, calm and dense in both. This is an Operate-mode surface that stays open for hours, so the brand lives in precise details: a 28px row unit shared by every list row, tool row and panel header; one display face reserved for the names of agents and speakers, and a lowercase mono wordmark shared with nerve.zone; prose held to a measure that spans the column. State changes are a light switch, 120ms, on or off. Both worlds refuse the hacker costume (scanlines, glowing HUD chrome, tracked uppercase labels), the grey dashboard with one decorative accent, glass and decorative gradients, coloured side rails, and any pulsing or breathing motion. The retired community themes and Classic skin do not return; these four themes are the whole set.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Light temperature is the state vocabulary in both worlds: blue works, cantaloupe waits on you, red failed, dark is done.
- Panes are the state marker: an idle pane shows its family colour at 84% strength, a lit pane switches to the state colour; Night light adds a short bloom, Workbench switches it flat. No dots, rails or glyph icons.
- One 28px row unit across list rows, tool rows, panel headers and the status bar; the top bar is 44px.
- The shell is a dark or paper frame around one lifted room with a 6px reveal; tool stacks, code and the composer step up once more to Plate.
- Night light: plates read by value plus a 1px top edge light, and only floating surfaces cast a soft offset shadow. Workbench: plates are ruled cells, and floating surfaces print a hard 6px offset at 5% to 6%.
- Workbench pins the room sheet (and the login window) to the frame with four 7px diamond nodes, as rules cross on a drafting sheet.
- Corners stay between 1px and 4px; the Workbench room sheet is 2px.
- Night light sets Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for reading and UI, Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for measures, Geist for names. Workbench sets iA Writer Quattro S for reading and UI, IBM Plex Mono for measures, IBM Plex Serif for names. The wordmark is IBM Plex Mono in both.
- Sentence case everywhere in both worlds; no eyebrows, kickers or tracked caps.
- Motion is a 120ms switch with a near-linear ease-out; light travels down the spine 70ms per region when a run starts; a new turn lands with one 200ms rise; reduced motion makes all of it instant.
- The composer is the operator's next window: at rest a quiet plate with a dark gutter pane, warmed to the operator plate with a lit pane while the operator is in it.

## Colors

Two temperatures of light (blue for agent work, cantaloupe for the operator) and one red, on a neutral ground that changes by world: a deep blue-black night with warm reading white (Night), cool paper with ink (Daylight), a graphite drafting sheet with cool grey ink (Workbench Night), or pale drafting paper with graphite ink (Workbench Paper).

### Primary
- **Cantaloupe** (`cantaloupe` on the dark themes, `daylight-cantaloupe` on both paper themes, with `daylight-cantaloupe-text` for warm text on paper): the operator's light, in every world. It fills the Send button, the caret, the focus ring, text selection at 30%, the operator's pane, the operator plate's warm edge (an edge light in Night light, a drawn rule in Workbench), the composer border while the operator is inside it, and the hover colour of link underlines. It also marks finished work waiting on the operator (unread sessions, fleet pane). It never appears as decoration or on an agent's output.

### Secondary
- **Electric Blue** (`electric-blue`, Night) and **Workbench Cobalt** (`wb-cobalt`, Workbench Night; `wb-paper-cobalt`, Workbench Paper): agent work in progress. Lights a pane while a session runs, a tool row while it executes, the streaming cursor, the file the agent is touching in the workspace tree, and the fleet pane beside the wordmark. Live measures and elapsed clocks take the text tone so they pass on the panel: **Electric Blue Text** (`electric-blue-text`) in Night, the pale **Cobalt Ink** (`wb-cobalt-text`) in Workbench Night; on paper the cobalt is its own ink. Daylight uses `daylight-blue`.

### Tertiary
- **Signal Red** (`signal-red` on both dark themes; `daylight-red` in Daylight; `wb-paper-red` in Workbench Paper): failure only. An error pane, a failed message's marker, destructive buttons and their hover border. It is the destructive token as well, so there is one red per theme.
- **Success Green** (`success-green` in Night, `wb-green` in Workbench Night, the Daylight green on both paper themes): confined to diff additions, copy-confirmed feedback and the new-file view. Never a state pane.

### Neutral

Night light, Night:
- **Midnight** (`midnight`): the ground and the frame. The top bar, both side columns, the status bar, inputs and keycaps sit on it.
- **Panel** (`panel`): one step up, the room. The centre column (chat header, transcript, composer band) is one lifted plate of Panel inside the Midnight frame; the segmented track in the top bar also sits on it.
- **Plate** (`plate`): one more step, for surfaces inside the room: tool stacks, code blocks and the composer.
- **Raised** (`raised`): the hover and selected fill for rows, chips, icon buttons and the active chip on the segmented track; also inline code background.
- **Popover** (`popover`): floating menus, the command palette and inline select listboxes.
- **Rule** (`rule`): hairline dividers, panel borders and the tool stack's row separators. It is kept low so plates read by value, not by lines.
- **Input Edge** (`input-edge`): the one stroke that is allowed to be drawn: text inputs, outline buttons and inline select triggers at rest.
- **Reading White** (`reading-white`): all primary text and the wordmark; warm so prose does not glare at night.
- **Slate** (`slate`): secondary text, chip labels at rest, tool arguments, metadata and placeholders.
- **Pane Off** and **Pane Edge** (`pane-off`, `pane-edge`): a dark window with a 1px inset edge; Pane Edge is also the blockquote rule, link underline at rest, and the active toolbar button's border.
- **Operator Plate** (`operator-plate`): Panel warmed by roughly 15% cantaloupe, the ground of the operator's message.
- **Scrollbar** (`scrollbar`): a quiet rule, never a feature.

Night light, Daylight (`daylight-*`) keeps every role and swaps the values: paper ground, white panels, ink text, a darker blue and cantaloupe text tone so both pass on paper, and pane edges that print as a visible hairline.

Workbench, Workbench Night (`wb-*`), the same roles on a graphite drafting sheet:
- **Graphite** (`wb-graphite`): the frame, inputs, keycaps and the Send label.
- **Graphite Panel** (`wb-panel`): the room sheet and side columns; also the fill of an off pane and of the corner nodes.
- **Graphite Plate** (`wb-plate`): tool stacks, code blocks, the composer, popovers and muted grounds.
- **Graphite Raised** (`wb-raised`): hover and selected fills, secondary and accent grounds.
- **Drawn Rule** (`wb-rule`): rules are structure in this world, not hairlines kept out of sight; also the scrollbar.
- **Field Edge** (`wb-field-edge`): inputs, outline buttons and select triggers, lifted from the source system's edge to reach 3:1 on every Workbench surface.
- **Workbench Ink** (`wb-ink`) and **Workbench Muted** (`wb-muted`): primary and secondary text; cooler and quieter than Reading White.
- **Node Edge Pane** (`wb-pane-edge`): the frame of an off pane, which reads as a drafting node.
- **Graphite Operator Plate** (`wb-operator-plate`): the panel warmed by 16% cantaloupe, the ground of the operator's message and of the composer while the operator is in it.
- **Corner Node Edge** (`wb-node-edge`) and **Key Edge** (`wb-key-edge`): the outline of the corner nodes and the pressed lower edge of the Send key.

Workbench Paper (`wb-paper-*`), the source theme of Ruled Workbench: a pale paper frame (`wb-paper`), a near-white room (`wb-paper-panel`), Plate (`wb-paper-plate`), white popovers and off panes (`wb-paper-white`), Raised (`wb-paper-raised`), drawn rules (`wb-paper-rule`), field edges darkened to 3:1 (`wb-paper-field-edge`), graphite ink and muted text (`wb-paper-ink`, `wb-paper-muted`), a pane edge (`wb-paper-pane-edge`), the operator plate warmed by 14% cantaloupe (`wb-paper-operator-plate`), the Send label (`wb-paper-send-label`), and the node and key edges (`wb-paper-node-edge`, `wb-paper-key-edge`).

### Identity
Six hues name agent families, not states, identically in both worlds: Mint, Lilac, Aqua, Rose, Lemon and Sky (`identity-*`). Cantaloupe is the operator's light and never an agent's, the main agent included, so a warm name or pane always means the operator or work waiting on them; a browser that remembered cantaloupe for an agent forgets it and the agent takes a free hue. A family's hue is assigned on first sight, least-used first, remembered in the browser, and adjustable by the operator from a root row's actions. Subagents and cron children take lighter shades of the parent hue (62% and 44% of the hue mixed toward white). Neither blue (Electric Blue or Workbench Cobalt) is in this list, so an idle blue family can never be mistaken for work in progress.

### Named Rules
**The Light Temperature Rule.** In every world, blue is agent work, cantaloupe is the operator and anything waiting on the operator, red is error, and nothing else is coloured. A surface that is not reporting one of those three states is a neutral. Family hues are identity, not state: they sit on idle panes, on names, on the header band and on the open row, and a pane that is lit always shows the state colour.

**The Identity Rule.** Every agent family owns one of six hues (never Cantaloupe, which is the operator's; children take a lighter shade of their root's hue), assigned on first sight and operator-pickable from a root row. The hue stays on at idle: 84% of it fills the family's off panes (70% in the frame) except in history lists (The Dark History Rule), the agent's name takes it in the display face (darkened 42% toward ink on the paper themes), the chat header band takes a 9% tint (14% on paper) and the open room's row a 14% tint over Raised. Blue is never an identity hue, and a lit pane always shows the state colour, so identity and state never trade places.

**The Dark History Rule.** A list of finished work (Done today, Since you looked, a finished subagent under its parent) keeps every pane dark: Pane Off with its edge, no family hue in the glass. The family hue moves to the agent's name on the row, so who did it still reads at a glance while nothing in the list looks lit. Only a row whose state still matters lights its pane: a cron still failing or a run that ended in failure shows red, a chat waiting on the operator shows Cantaloupe.

**The Window Rule.** The composer is the operator's next window. At rest it is a quiet plate with a dark pane in the gutter; while the operator is in it (focus, or text already typed) it warms to the operator plate with a warm edge (an edge light in Night light, a 55% cantaloupe rule in Workbench) and the pane lights Cantaloupe, so the turn being written looks like the turns already sent. The side-column editors are windows too: the memory editor and the config editor sit on Plate and, while the operator is in them, warm the same way while the small pane in their status line lights Cantaloupe; no focus ring.

**The Lifted Cobalt Rule.** Workbench's cobalt is lifted where the source system's deep fill would fail: a lit pane on Workbench Night takes Workbench Cobalt and live text takes Cobalt Ink, and field edges on both Workbench themes are raised to 3:1. Never use the source's darker cobalt or lighter field edge on a Workbench surface.

## Typography

Night light:
**Display Font:** Geist (with Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Helvetica Neue, Arial)
**Body Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (with Helvetica Neue, Arial)
**Label/Mono Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono (with JetBrains Mono, Fira Code, Cascadia Code, Consolas)

Workbench:
**Display Font:** IBM Plex Serif (with Georgia), from Google Fonts at 400, 500 and 600
**Body Font:** iA Writer Quattro S (with IBM Plex Sans, Helvetica Neue, Arial), self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License in regular, italic, bold and bold italic
**Label/Mono Font:** IBM Plex Mono (with ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas), from Google Fonts

**Character:** In Night light, Atkinson carries the whole console at a 15px base with kerning, ligatures and contextual alternates on; Geist appears only where a name is spoken, always at 700 with tight tracking, and the mono shares Atkinson's skeleton. In Workbench, Quattro reads and runs the UI set tight (-0.025em on body and control text, as in the source), Plex Serif at semibold and normal tracking names things, and Plex Mono measures at normal tracking. Workbench's faces load only while a Workbench theme is active. An explicit font pick in Appearance still overrides the reading face in either world. Both worlds use tabular numerals for counts and clocks so they do not shimmer. The wordmark sits outside both worlds: lowercase `nerve` in IBM Plex Mono at regular weight, self-hosted as the Nerve Wordmark face so it is the same in every theme and on nerve.zone and the docs.

### Hierarchy
Sizes, line heights and roles are shared by both worlds; the face and tracking follow the world (frontmatter `nameplate` through `keycap` for Night light, `wb-*` for Workbench; `wordmark` is shared).
- **Wordmark** (1.333rem, line-height 1; IBM Plex Mono 400 at -0.02em in every theme): lowercase `nerve`, after the Nerve mark and followed by the 12x17 fleet pane. The same lockup as nerve.zone and docs.nerve.zone.
- **Nameplate** (1.2rem, line-height 1.1; Geist 700 at -0.02em, or Plex Serif 600 at normal tracking): the open session's agent name in the chat header, in its family hue; the one place a name is set larger than the text around it.
- **Name** (inherits size in rows and message headers; Geist 700 or 600 at -0.01em, or Plex Serif 600 at normal tracking): agent and speaker names in top-level session rows and message headers. An agent's name takes its family hue; the operator's name stays in the primary text colour on its warm plate. A reply is signed by the agent that wrote it: a root or subagent session by its own name, and every other session of an agent (a direct or channel conversation, a group or thread, a cron job) by the family's root name, never by a session label that names a peer, a room or a job.
- **Headline** (600, 1.2rem, 1.2): section titles on settings and dialog surfaces.
- **Title** (600, 0.867rem): panel header labels, field labels, Send, table heads.
- **Body** (400, 1rem, 1.6): agent prose, held to the 72em reading measure (about 140 characters, which is the whole column on most screens). Code blocks, tables and charts may take the full column.
- **UI** (500, 0.867rem): chips, list rows, inputs, toolbar buttons, tree entries.
- **Label** (500, 0.8rem): status bar, metadata, hints, badges, intermediate assistant messages at 0.7 opacity.
- **Mono** (400, 0.8rem, tabular): tool rows, timestamps, durations, model names, inline select menus and code block language bars. Keycaps drop to 0.733rem.

Quattro ships regular and bold only, so in Workbench the 500 roles (UI, Label) render at regular and the 600 roles (Headline, Title) at bold.

### Named Rules
**The Names Rule.** The display face (Geist in Night light, Plex Serif in Workbench) is used only for agent or speaker names. The wordmark has its own mono lockup. Headings, labels and buttons stay in the reading face.

**The Sentence Case Rule.** Every label, header, chip and button is sentence case in both worlds. No eyebrows, no kickers above headings, no letterspaced capitals; Workbench's uppercase eyebrows from the landing page are not adopted. A kicker includes the run-in label: text never opens with "What happened:", "Result:" or "Summary:" ahead of what it is; the place it sits says what it is.

**The Tight Hand Rule.** In Workbench, Quattro body and control text tracks -0.025em; code, measures, dividers, the fleet in words, the editor and names keep normal tracking. An explicit tracking utility still wins.

## Layout

Layout is shared by both worlds. Three columns on one ground, no outer chrome. A 44px top bar carries the Nerve mark, the wordmark and fleet pane, the Chat and Tasks segmented track, usage and settings. Below it the workspace tree sits left, the transcript and composer in the centre, and the agents column right with the session rows and then Memory, Crons, Tasks and Config. A 28px status bar closes the frame with connection pane and counts in words. Column widths are user-resizable (ResizablePanels stores percentages), and the layout collapses to a single column with panel toggles at 900px.

Vertical rhythm is the row unit: 1.867rem (28px at the 15px base) for every list row, tool row, panel header, slash menu item and the status bar. Controls that hold text input use 2.133rem (32px); on viewports at or under 640px chips, icon buttons and inputs grow to a 44px minimum and inputs go to 16px text so mobile browsers do not zoom. Horizontal insets are 12px on panel headers and plates, 10px on chips and tool rows, 4px to 8px between siblings.

In the transcript every message, tool stack and plate shares one left text edge. The state pane sits in a 24px gutter to the left of that edge, so the lit panes form a spine down the column. Prose is capped at the 72em reading measure, which is the whole column on most screens; the transcript and the composer span the column edge to edge.

### Named Rules
**The One Row Rule.** Every list row, tool row, panel header and the status bar sits on the 1.867rem (28px) row unit in both worlds; density comes from sharing one rhythm, not from shrinking it.

## Elevation & Depth

Both worlds are tonal: depth comes from stepping value (frame, room, Plate inside the room, Raised for hover and selection, Popover for anything floating). They differ in how a surface shows its edge and how a floating surface leaves the ground.

Night light has one lighting trick. A raised plate does not draw a border; it carries a 1px highlight along its top edge (the edge light) so it reads as a lit surface against the ground. Only surfaces that truly float (the composer, popovers, menus, dialogs, the command palette) cast a real soft offset shadow, and they keep the edge light too. A lit pane throws a bloom, and the room carries an ambient light from above. In Daylight there is no edge to catch light, so the plate token becomes a printed hairline in the rule colour, the float shadow softens to an ink tint, and there is no bloom and no room light.

Workbench is printed, not lit. Every plate is a ruled cell (a 1px inset rule in the drawn rule colour); there is no edge light, no bloom, no room light and no blur. Floating chrome (menus, popovers, dialogs, drawers, the command palette, a task card in the operator's hand) prints a hard 6px offset in a few percent of cobalt; surfaces that rest (the composer, the login window, task cards at rest) are ruled cells with no offset. The room takes a cool wash from the top, and paper grain lies over the whole shell, including the top bar.

Floating chrome in both worlds reads its shadow from the theme (the menu and dialog shadow tokens), so a world decides whether chrome blurs or prints.

### Shadow Vocabulary
Night light:
- **Edge light** (`box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.055)`): every plate, tool stack and the active chip on the segmented track. Daylight: `inset 0 0 0 1px var(--border)`.
- **Warm edge light** (`box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-operator) 30%, transparent)`): the operator's message plate. Daylight: `inset 0 0 0 1px color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-operator) 45%, var(--border))`.
- **Float** (`box-shadow: 0 14px 36px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.46), 0 1px 2px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.4), inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.07)`): the composer and the command palette shell. Daylight: `0 14px 36px rgba(19, 23, 34, 0.14), 0 1px 2px rgba(19, 23, 34, 0.08), inset 0 0 0 1px var(--border)`.
- **Menu** (`box-shadow: 0 12px 32px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.32)`): inline select listboxes, slash menus, popovers, top bar panels, toasts, confirmations, the cron dialog, the image lightbox and the mobile column drawers.
- **Dialog** (`box-shadow: 0 18px 48px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.38)`): the shared modal dialog (Create task, Add memory and the other form dialogs built on it).
- **Pane edge** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px var(--color-pane-edge)`): a dark pane's window frame; it drops to none when the pane lights. Shared by both worlds.
- **Bloom** (`box-shadow: 0 0 9px 0 color-mix(in srgb, <state colour> 58% to 62%, transparent)`): the light a lit pane throws on the ground, in the pane's own colour; Daylight and both Workbench themes set it to none.
- **Room light** (`radial-gradient(120% 70% at 50% 0%, 8% live blue in Panel, Panel 60%)`): the transcript ground's ambient light from above; Daylight has none.

Workbench:
- **Ruled cell** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px var(--color-border)`): every plate, tool stack, task card at rest, the composer at rest and the login window.
- **Print** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px var(--color-border), 6px 6px 0 0 rgba(110, 150, 255, 0.06)`; Paper `rgba(7, 77, 207, 0.05)`): the float token, used by the command palette shell, the Settings and task drawers and a lifted task card.
- **Menu and dialog print** (`box-shadow: 6px 6px 0 0 rgba(110, 150, 255, 0.06)`; Paper `rgba(7, 77, 207, 0.05)`): everything that takes the menu or dialog token (see Night light above).
- **Warm rule** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-operator) 14%, var(--color-border))`): the operator's message plate.
- **Window rule** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px color-mix(in srgb, var(--color-operator) 55%, var(--color-border))`): the composer and the side-column editors while the operator is in them.
- **Key edge** (`box-shadow: inset 0 -2px 0 0 var(--wb-key-edge)`): the pressed lower edge of the Send key; it drops on hover and press.
- **Keycap edge** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 0 var(--color-input)`): the keycap keeps only its lower edge.
- **Cool wash** (`linear-gradient(to bottom, rgba(40, 80, 220, 0.08), transparent 28%)`; Paper `rgba(219, 234, 254, 0.16)`): the room's ground, in place of the room light.
- **Grain** (fractal noise tile, 180px, at 2.5% on Night with the specks inverted to light, 3% on Paper): one overlay over the shell, never on individual surfaces.

### Named Rules
**The Edge Light Rule.** In Night, plates read by value and a 1px top highlight, not by drawn borders; Daylight prints a hairline instead. Drawn borders are reserved for inputs, outline buttons and hairline dividers.

**The Frame Rule.** The shell is a frame around one lifted room, in both worlds. Top bar, side columns and status bar sit flat on the frame with rules only; the centre column is a single plate of Panel and the frame shows for 6px around it. Surfaces inside the room step up once more to Plate. Nothing else in the shell is a plate.

**The Printed Rule.** In Workbench nothing is lit: no edge light, no bloom, no room light, no blur, not even on the command palette's scrim. A plate is a ruled cell. Only floating chrome prints an offset, always hard, always 6px down and right, always at 5% to 6% cobalt; a surface that rests never prints.

**The Switch Rule.** State changes switch in 120ms with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`; nothing breathes or pulses except two signs of live work: the Nerve mark in the top bar, which fires spoke by spoke (a 2.4s CSS cycle) while any agent works, and the live horizon behind the composer while the open session works (see Live horizon). Reduced motion makes every transition and animation instant. Light travels: when a run starts, the fleet pane beside the wordmark comes on first, the chat header 70ms later, the agents column at 140ms and the transcript gutter at 210ms, so state arrives as one sweep down the spine; lights go out together. Arrivals (drawers, menus, the lifted chip crossing its track) use the arrive ease `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` at 180ms to 260ms, never a bounce. The Chat and Tasks switch is a view transition: the lifted chip slides across the segmented track in 220ms while the room cross-fades in 160ms. On send the composer lifts 3px and settles in 220ms as the turn leaves it and lands in the transcript. Entrances (messages, menus, drawers) fade or slide once in 140ms to 220ms using the same ease; a new turn in the transcript lands with one 200ms rise of 6px and a fade, and that is the transcript's only entrance. Motion is shared by both worlds.

**The Bloom Rule.** In Night, a lit pane casts a short bloom of its own colour, and that is the only light in the system that spreads: off panes, plates, buttons and text never glow. The bloom switches on and off with the pane in 120ms. Daylight and Workbench print panes flat.

## Shapes

Corners are tight and deliberate in both worlds. Panes are 1px, keycaps and badges 2px, buttons, chips, inputs, plates and tool stacks 3px, the composer and segmented track 4px, and the command palette shell 6px. The room plate is 4px in Night light and 2px in Workbench, where it reads as a ruled sheet. The radius scale is clamped: every larger step resolves to 4px so legacy utilities cannot round a panel again, and shell panels are square. Panes are upright rectangles, taller than wide (10x14), never circles or dots, and never carry a glyph; in Workbench an off pane reads as a drafting node, a panel-filled cell inside its node-edge frame.

Night light borders are hairlines in the rule colour or the input edge colour, 1px, used for dividers, inputs and outline controls; everything else is a plate, a flat fill with an edge light. Workbench draws its rules as structure: the same 1px weight, but every plate is a ruled cell and the rules are meant to be seen. Content inside stacks is clipped to the stack's radius in both worlds.

Workbench's one added form is the diamond corner node: a square turned 45 degrees, a 7px fill in the panel colour over an 11px outline in the node edge colour, centred where the room's rules meet the frame.

## Components

Flat, exact, and the same in both worlds. Controls are quiet fills that step to Raised on hover; state is shown by the lit pane next to them, never by a coloured background on the control itself. Where a world changes a component's material, the entry says so.

### Pane (signature component)
The state marker and the whole brand in one element. An inline rectangle, 0.667rem by 0.933rem (10x14), radius 1px. Off: Pane Off fill with a 1px inset Pane Edge frame (in Workbench, the panel fill inside the node edge, a drafting node). Live: the world's blue, frame removed. Operator: Cantaloupe. Error: the world's red. Small (0.533rem by 0.733rem, 8x11) sits on text lines: tool rows, message headers, the composer's gutter, composer voice state. The chat header's run state uses the default size; the brand pane beside the wordmark is 0.8rem by 1.133rem (12x17). A system line carries a dark pane, or red when the line reports a failure (a turn that ended before a reply, a send or reset error, a history load error). Transitions are background and box-shadow over 120ms; colour is never the only signal, so a pane with no adjacent text carries an accessible label (Running, Waiting on you, Failed, Idle). A pane that belongs to an agent family carries its identity hue at 84% while off (see The Identity Rule) and switches to the state colour when lit, with its bloom in Night and flat in Daylight and Workbench.


### Corner nodes (Workbench signature component)
The room is pinned to the frame like a drafted sheet. Four diamond nodes sit on the corners of the centre column where its rules meet the frame, and on the login window's own corners: an 11px diamond in Corner Node Edge with a 7px diamond in the panel colour on top (white on Paper), drawn above content, never interactive. They appear only in Workbench, and only on the room sheet and the login window.

### Live horizon

The room's sign that the open session is working: a faint segmented equalizer rising from the floor of the chat column behind the composer and under the transcript text, the landing page hero's meter brought indoors (`src/features/chat/LiveHorizon.tsx`, `.nerve-horizon`). Bars in the theme's live blue (Electric Blue in Night light, cobalt in Workbench), 10px wide on a 14px pitch, cut into 10px segments with 4px gaps, up to 12 segments (168px) tall; taller toward the column edges, shorter in the middle where the composer covers them. The layer sits at 20% (14% on the paper themes) and fades out upward, so it shows in the composer's gutters and a few segments above it and reads from the corner of the eye. Bars step between heights with no easing on 5.5s to 8.5s cycles, a meter rather than a pulse, and move by scaleY only. It comes on with the room's 210ms light delay and goes out at once when the run ends. When the session is idle, in performance mode or under reduced motion it is not in the DOM; the chat header's pane still says Running.

### Buttons
- **Shape:** tight corners (3px), 1px transparent border reserved for outline and secondary variants, 120ms transition on background, border, colour and filter, 1px downward press on active.
- **Primary (Send):** Cantaloupe fill with a dark label (Midnight in Night, Graphite in Workbench Night, the ink of the paper theme), 600 weight at 0.867rem, 0 by 0.875rem padding, 2rem tall (2.75rem on touch viewports). Hover raises brightness 10%; a sent message flashes it brighter for 200ms; a send error shakes it 2px. It is the one warm control on the screen; on a decision screen, where Send is not the point, the warm fill moves to the one decision the operator should make first (The One Warm Control Rule). In Workbench it is a key: a 2px pressed lower edge in Key Edge at rest, which drops on hover, and a press settles to 99% scale.
- **Hover / Focus:** every variant takes a 2px Cantaloupe outline offset 1px on focus-visible. Disabled drops to 50% opacity.
- **Outline:** transparent fill, input edge stroke (Field Edge in Workbench), primary text, Raised on hover. **Ghost:** no stroke, secondary text, Raised fill and primary text on hover. **Destructive:** red fill with a dark label. **Link:** in prose, the live text tone with an underline at 38% that fills on hover; in chrome, primary text underlined in Pane Edge.
- **Toolbar button:** row-unit tall, rule stroke, primary text; active state takes Raised fill and a Pane Edge stroke.

### Chips
- **Shell chip:** transparent, secondary text at 0.867rem 500, row-unit tall, 0 by 10px padding, 3px radius. Hover and active both switch to Raised fill with primary text. No stroke, no accent colour.
- **Segmented track:** a Panel channel on the frame with 2px padding and 4px radius holding chips 6px shorter than the row unit; the active chip is lifted onto the track with Raised fill and the plate token (edge light in Night, a ruled cell in Workbench).
- **Badge:** 1.333rem tall, 2px radius, rule stroke, secondary text at 0.8rem. Tones tint the stroke by mixing 45% of the state colour into the rule: live takes the live text tone, primary and warning take cantaloupe text, danger takes red.

### Cards / Containers
- **Corner Style:** 3px for plates and tool stacks, 4px for the composer, 6px for the command palette; the room plate is 4px in Night light and 2px in Workbench.
- **Background:** Plate inside the room; Popover for floating menus.
- **Shadow Strategy:** the plate token on plates (edge light in Night, hairline in Daylight, ruled cell in Workbench); the float token on the command palette and drawers; the menu token on listboxes, popovers and confirmations; the dialog token on the shared modal dialog (see Elevation & Depth).
- **Border:** none on Night light plates; Workbench plates are ruled cells. Panel headers have a rule below. Code blocks and tool result details keep a rule because they sit inside prose.
- **Internal Padding:** 12px horizontal on headers and plates, 8px 12px on message plates, 10px 12px on code blocks.
- **Operator plate:** the operator's whole turn (name, time and body) sits on the theme's operator plate, 3px radius, no border, width fit to content and capped at the reading measure. Night light gives it the warm edge light; Workbench gives it the warm rule (14% cantaloupe into the drawn rule). The small pane in the gutter is Cantaloupe; the speaker name stays in the primary text colour in the display face, since the plate already says who is speaking.
- **Tool stack:** consecutive tool rows share one plate, clipped to 3px, divided by rules; edge light in Night, ruled cell in Workbench.
- **Code block:** Plate with a rule and a row-unit language bar. Syntax hues stay off the live blue and cantaloupe, which carry state: on the dark themes keywords take Lilac, titles and tags Aqua, numbers and attributes a pale rose, strings a pale green and built-ins a pale sand, comments italic; on the paper themes the same roles take darker inks. Workbench keeps those hues and takes its own comment and heading tones (Workbench Muted and a near-white heading on Night, Workbench Paper Muted and black on Paper). The code editor follows the same palette per world.

### Inputs / Fields
- **Style:** frame-colour fill, 1px input edge stroke (Field Edge in Workbench, held at 3:1), 3px radius, 2.133rem tall (2.75rem on touch), 0 by 10px padding, UI size text, secondary placeholder.
- **Focus:** stroke turns Cantaloupe and a 1px Cantaloupe outline sits flush on it; no glow.
- **Error / Disabled:** aria-invalid turns the stroke red; disabled drops to 50% opacity and refuses the pointer.
- **Composer:** the operator's next window. A plate of Plate, 4px radius, a small pane in the gutter to its left and a 1px border that is transparent at rest. In Night light it floats with the float shadow; in Workbench it rests as a ruled cell with no offset. While the operator is in it (focus, or text already typed) the plate warms to the operator plate (warm edge light in Night light, the 55% cantaloupe window rule in Workbench), the border lights Cantaloupe at 60% (solid Cantaloupe while recording) and the gutter pane switches to Cantaloupe, so the turn being written matches the turns already sent (The Window Rule). The textarea is borderless at 1rem on a 42px minimum height; a toolbar row below holds shell icon buttons on the left, shortcut hints as keycaps on the right, and Send at the end. The composer shares the transcript's gutter and spans the column with it.
- **Inline select trigger:** transparent, input edge stroke, 3px radius, 0.8rem text, 1.75rem tall on desktop (2.75rem on touch), Raised on hover, chevron in the secondary text colour. Its listbox is Popover with a rule stroke, the menu token and 0.667rem mono options.
- **Keycap:** inline flex, 1.333rem tall, 0 by 5px padding, 2px radius, rule stroke, frame fill, mono at 0.733rem in the secondary text colour. Used for composer shortcut hints and nowhere decorative. In Night light a hairline sits beneath it and a thread of light on top, so it reads as a raised cap; in Workbench it keeps only the lower edge.

A task is a row on Plate with the plate token (edge light in Night, ruled cell in Workbench), Raised on hover: its pane leads (blue while its agent runs, red when the run broke, Cantaloupe while it waits in Review, the assignee's family hue in the glass otherwise), then the title in primary text, one line of description, the labels as words, and who holds it (the agent's name in its hue, the operator as "You"), the priority as a word (Critical in red, High in primary text, Low muted, Normal silent) and the run in a word. No pills, no dots, no badges. Only the card in the operator's hand casts the float token (a soft shadow in Night light, the hard print in Workbench). The task drawer is the Panel drawer of Settings: the task's pane and status in the header, a flat form with the description as an operator window, Details, Agent run (its own pane), Result and Feedback as sections on Plate, and the workflow actions in the footer with Execute and Approve as the warm primary. The quick view in the workspace column uses the column's row idiom: a pane leads each task, the assignee closes the row.
- **Workspace column (Memory, Crons, Config):** one underline tab strip on the row unit, then flat rows on the frame with Raised on hover; no row borders. ### Attention (Now and Today)
The operator's catch-up surface in the centre room, a Now | Today tab pair on its heading row. At the desk both views use one grid once the room is 54rem wide: a wide work column and a 22rem rail with a 24px gap (`.attention-grid`, `minmax(0, 1fr) 22rem`); phones stack it in one column. Rows are the list row idiom (44px at the desk, 60px on phones), open in place, and while any row is open the order holds still.

- **Now (an urgency board):** the heading is the counts in words with the numbers in mono ("1 failing · 3 to decide · 2 working": red, operator and live text), and says "Nothing needs you" when that is true. Failing comes first and holds only what is failing by its latest state (a cron whose newest run failed, a failed task or session), each with Retry or Run now inline. Waiting on you follows: the top decision sits on the Start here plate (the operator plate, the board's one warm surface, carrying the only warm filled button), and every other decision is a row that opens in place to what happened in its thread, its evidence, every action and Reply. The rail holds the Agents strip (each agent's current work in a line, for how long, quiet runs with Stop) and Since you looked (collapsed, finished items only, never a successful cron run, one Mark all seen). The Ask box docks at the bottom of the work column like the chat composer. An action applies at once and leaves an undo receipt in the item's place ("Approved 'Rotate the API keys' · Undo 5s"); the request goes out when the window closes, and what was handled is listed at the bottom of Now. Items are named in words (an agent, a task title, a cron job), never by session key.
- **Today (the day so far):** the heading is the date with Refresh; under it one quiet line of facts in muted text with mono numbers ("9 chats · 3 done · 1 merged · 1 failing"), each a text button that filters its rows or jumps to them, and a short model-written paragraph that leads with failures and never mentions cron runs that went fine. Chats today takes the wide column and leads (open on phones too): every chat active today with its real name, channel, the first sentence of its summary shown whole, and a turn marker at the right, "Waiting on you" in operator text when the Now board holds it or its agent's last message asks the operator something (with Reply as its outline action), "Working" in live text while it runs, nothing otherwise. Subagents nest under their parent with how they ended. Done today takes the rail (collapsed on phones): crons still failing pinned on top with Run now, then tasks with the pull requests they merged, then a failure that recovered as one line ("Failed 09:00, recovered 12:03"), then every successful cron run folded into one quiet line ("18 cron runs ok") that opens to a row per job. It is a timeline: a mono time column printed once when rows share a minute, no hour headers, and an open row's detail starts on the title's edge. Nothing on Today repeats a row: a subagent lives only under its chat, a merged pull request only on its task. Open sections are remembered per browser.

### Tool row
One 28px line of mono text at 0.8rem: a small pane (live while running, off when done, red when failed), the verb in primary text capped at 45% width, the arguments in the secondary colour, and a right-aligned tabular measure that turns the live text tone while live. Expandable rows take Raised on hover and focus and end with a 14px chevron in the secondary colour. Identical in both worlds apart from the faces.

### Named Rules
**The One Warm Control Rule.** A screen has one warm filled control. In the chat it is Send; on a decision screen it is the decision the operator should make first (the Start here plate's main action on Now). Every other action on that screen, however important, is an outline button or text, so the eye lands once.

**The Action Tier Rule.** In lists an item's main action is an outline button (field edge, primary text, 28px at the desk, 44px on phones), every second action is muted underlined text, and opening the item's source is text too. A chip is never an item's main action; chips stay for quiet view controls like Refresh.

**The Default Chrome Rule.** Source, freshness and ranking chrome ("Checked 14:16", "Ranked by...", a live source named live) appears only when it is not the default: a source that could not be read, a source turned off, a count that is a floor. A normal view carries no meta line at all.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** show state only with a lit pane, in both worlds: blue for agent work, Cantaloupe for the operator and work waiting on the operator, red for failure, Pane Off when done (The Light Temperature Rule).
- **Do** keep an agent family's hue on at idle: its panes at 84%, its name, the header band and the open row; lit panes always show the state colour (The Identity Rule).
- **Do** switch panes and hover fills in 120ms with `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` and let reduced motion make them instant (The Switch Rule).
- **Do** warm the composer to the operator plate and light its gutter pane while the operator is in it, on focus or with text typed, so the turn being written matches the turns already sent (The Window Rule).
- **Do** treat the side-column editors as operator windows: Plate at rest, the operator plate with a lit status-line pane while the operator types, never a focus ring (The Window Rule).
- **Do** let a cron row's pane carry the job's light: blue in flight, red while an enabled job stands broken, off otherwise; a skipped run stays quiet.
- **Do** let a new turn land with one 200ms rise and fade on the switch ease; the transcript has no other entrance.
- **Do** let light travel: stagger a state's arrival by region (fleet pane, chat header, agents column, transcript gutter, 70ms apart) and never by element; lights go out together.
- **Do** move chrome on the arrive ease (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, 180ms to 260ms) and switch views with a view transition; no bounce, no elastic curves.
- **Do** build raised surfaces from the plate token so each world draws its own edge: the 1px edge light in Night, a printed hairline in Daylight, a ruled cell in Workbench (The Edge Light Rule, The Printed Rule, The Frame Rule).
- **Do** give floating chrome the menu or dialog shadow token rather than a fixed shadow, so Workbench can print it (The Printed Rule).
- **Do** keep every list row, tool row, panel header and the status bar on the 1.867rem row unit (The One Row Rule).
- **Do** reserve the display face (Geist in Night light, Plex Serif 600 in Workbench) for agent or speaker names, and set the wordmark only in its lowercase Plex Mono lockup (The Names Rule).
- **Do** set Quattro body and control text at -0.025em in Workbench and keep code, measures and names at normal tracking (The Tight Hand Rule).
- **Do** hold agent prose to the 72em reading measure and let code, tables and charts take the column.
- **Do** set timestamps, counts, durations, models and code in the world's mono (Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono or IBM Plex Mono) with tabular numerals.
- **Do** keep corners between 1px and 4px; 6px is only for the floating command palette shell, and the Workbench room sheet is 2px.
- **Do** make Send the one warm control on a screen: Cantaloupe fill, dark label, 3px radius; in Workbench it carries the 2px pressed lower edge. On a decision screen the warm fill belongs to the first decision instead, and nothing else is warm (The One Warm Control Rule).
- **Do** give a list item's main action an outline button and every other action muted underlined text (The Action Tier Rule).
- **Do** keep panes dark in history lists and put the family hue on the agent's name (The Dark History Rule).
- **Do** pin the Workbench room sheet and login window with the four diamond corner nodes, and nothing else.
- **Do** use Workbench's lifted cobalt and the 3:1 field edges on Workbench surfaces (The Lifted Cobalt Rule).
- **Do** give every pane without adjacent text an accessible label (Running, Waiting on you, Failed, Idle).

### Don't:
- **Don't** colour anything that is not reporting agent work, operator ownership, an error, a link, code syntax or an agent family's identity; no decorative accents or coloured side rails.
- **Don't** pulse, breathe or animate a shadow; in Night the only glow is a lit pane's bloom (The Bloom Rule), Workbench has none, and the only blinking element is the streaming cursor pane, which stops under reduced motion.
- **Don't** add scanlines, HUD chrome, letterspaced uppercase labels, eyebrows, kickers above headings or run-in labels ("What happened:") in either world; Workbench's landing-page eyebrows stay on the landing page.
- **Don't** give an agent the Cantaloupe hue, the main agent included; warm always means the operator or work waiting on them.
- **Don't** show checked times, source names or ranking notes on a normal view; chrome appears only when something is not the default (The Default Chrome Rule).
- **Don't** use glass, blur or decorative gradients; the room light (Night) and the cool wash (Workbench) are the only gradients, the bloom is the only zero-offset shadow, and only floating surfaces cast an offset shadow.
- **Don't** draw a border around a Night light plate; there, borders belong to inputs, outline buttons and hairline dividers.
- **Don't** carry one world's material into the other: no edge light, bloom, room light or blur in Workbench, and no printed offset, grain, corner nodes or Plex faces in Night light.
- **Don't** let a Workbench print grow past 6px or 6%, soften it into a blur, or put it on a surface that rests (the composer, the login window, a task card at rest).
- **Don't** replace a pane with a dot, a circle, a badge or a glyph icon, and never make colour the only signal.
- **Don't** use the display face for headings, labels or buttons, or a system display face anywhere.
- **Don't** round panels or shell surfaces beyond 4px, or bring back the retired look's rounded panels and amber accents.
